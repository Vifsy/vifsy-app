# PATCH_V144_136.md

## Product posts – transparent-original compositing + exact visible copy

### What changed
- Product posts now keep native-transparent source products out of the GPT-image-2 generation canvas.
- For already transparent product images, GPT-image-2 generates the 4:5 background + typography composition first, and Spreelo composites the original exact product pixels back in afterward.
- This removes the earlier risk that GPT-image-2 both recreated a new product and showed the original, which could make the output look like duplicated garments/products.
- Product-post prompt text was updated so GPT-image-2 renders exact preselected visible copy instead of freely inventing all headline wording.
- The visible image copy now prioritizes:
  1. exact short headline from post content when suitable,
  2. exact verified product name/model,
  3. one exact supporting line when suitable.
- If no safe headline/supporting line is available, Spreelo omits it instead of forcing invented filler copy.

### Why
- Fix duplicated-product look for transparent-source product posts.
- Reduce spelling drift in generated on-image copy (for example accidental visual misspellings such as `tidllös`).
- Keep typography design free and premium while locking the actual words that should be rendered.

### Scope
- Affects editorial/product-post image generation in both:
  - normal AI Content Studio product posts
  - campaign/calendar product posts that use the same product-post generator

### Resulting flow
1. Product selected.
2. If source image already has real transparency:
   - GPT-image-2 generates a 4:5 editorial background/text composition without drawing the product.
   - Spreelo composites the original transparent product back into the reserved hero area.
3. If source image does **not** have real transparency:
   - GPT-image-2 recreates the product faithfully inside the final 4:5 editorial composition.
4. Text style remains AI-directed, but the actual visible words are now supplied explicitly by Spreelo whenever possible.
